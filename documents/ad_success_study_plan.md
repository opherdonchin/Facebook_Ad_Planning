# Slow Pairwise Meta Ad Criticism — Plan for Approval

**Status:** Revised proposal; no ad pair will be selected until this plan is approved.  
**Repository:** `D:\Repositories\Facebook_Ad_Planning`  
**Tracks:** men's campaign (`M`) and women's campaign (`W`), each with its own report and evolving interpretation.

## 1. The pace and form of the work

This will be a slow conversation, not a study completed behind the scenes.

In each round:

1. Choose **one clear winner and one clear loser** for the men's campaign.
2. Choose **one clear winner and one clear loser** for the women's campaign.
3. Produce two separate illustrated reports: one about the M pair and one about the W pair.
4. Stop and discuss the reports with the human.
5. Only after that discussion, decide whether another pair would be useful and what kind of pair it should be.

The two tracks may run in parallel, but they will not be forced into one explanation. A report may refer to the other audience when the comparison is genuinely illuminating.

There is no quota and no predetermined finish. The process continues only while there is clear signal or the next comparison feels creatively useful. It can pause after one round. It can continue on only one audience. It can end without a grand unified theory.

## 2. Stage 0 — Become a better-prepared creative critic

Before choosing the first pair, the agent will seek and acquire knowledge that improves the quality of the criticism.

This is not a search for a universal Meta checklist. It is an apprenticeship in several overlapping ways of seeing:

- direct-response advertising: attention, comprehension, belief, desire, and action;
- visual communication: composition, hierarchy, color, typography, layout, and image–word relationships;
- photographic and moving-image criticism: gesture, framing, authenticity, sequence, and emotional temperature;
- Meta-specific viewing conditions: mobile scale, placements, cropping, safe zones, feed behavior, and video openings;
- cultural and linguistic reading: Hebrew rhythm, right-to-left typography, gendered address, and the local meanings of wellness and martial-arts imagery.

### 2.1 Meta-search: map what knowledge is available

The first task is to map sources before treating any one source as authoritative:

- What official Meta creative training is public and current?
- Which materials are accessible directly to an agent, which require a login, and which are paywalled?
- What research is open access on visual attention, color, typography, congruence, and advertising response?
- Which respected creative-effectiveness frameworks disclose enough of their methods to be useful?
- What public ad libraries or galleries can supply instructive comparison material?
- What books, courses, reports, or account-specific screenshots would materially improve the reading if the human can provide them?

The search will privilege primary research, official platform material, and serious design education. Marketing blogs may help locate sources but will not become authorities merely because they offer confident lists.

### 2.2 Initial accessible source map

The preliminary search already found several useful categories:

- **Meta Blueprint:** public lessons on mobile creative, placement, format, and creative strategy.
- **Meta Ad Library:** a public, searchable collection of currently running ads that can provide contextual examples, although it does not reveal ordinary advertisers' performance.
- **Official Meta placement guidance:** current material on Reels format, sound, vertical composition, safe zones, and A/B testing.
- **Open research:** eye-tracking studies of typography, moving-image attention, color–text congruence, and visual appearance in online ads.
- **Open visual-design teaching:** material on visual hierarchy, typography, grids, color, contrast, negative space, and Gestalt organization.
- **Advertising-research organizations:** public methods and summaries from organizations that test attention and creative effectiveness. The agent will investigate these independently; the human does not need to know the organizations or have subscriptions. Vendor claims will be treated cautiously.

### 2.3 What the agent obtains, and the human's limited role

The burden of obtaining expertise belongs to the agent. The agent will first exhaust public platform guidance, open research, public design teaching, available advertising examples, and the repository itself.

The human is **not expected** to:

- know advertising-research organizations or have subscriptions;
- own advertising or design books;
- supply reference ads or professional aesthetic judgments;
- locate or trust a human advertising specialist.

The human's main contribution is the pair-by-pair conversation: whether an ad speaks to them, feels truthful to the dojo, sounds natural in Hebrew, or creates the wrong impression. Ordinary response is evidence for the critic; it does not need professional terminology.

If exact placement appearance becomes important, access will be considered in this order:

1. inspect the stored creative at realistic phone sizes and simulate likely crops;
2. ask for a specific screenshot only when it would resolve a named uncertainty;
3. with explicit permission, inspect Ads Manager through an already signed-in browser session;
4. only if necessary, use Meta's read-only ad-preview API with an existing repository or environment credential.

The human should not paste a long-lived access token into chat. A token will not be requested in advance. If API access becomes worthwhile, the agent will explain exactly what preview or metadata it would retrieve, request explicit permission, use a credential without displaying it, and avoid write operations.

The human will not be asked to buy a book speculatively. Free and open material comes first. If the source search identifies one book or paid source that would make a substantial difference, the agent may recommend it with a precise explanation of the missing knowledge and will wait for the human's decision.

No material will be fetched illicitly, and no external person will be contacted automatically.

### 2.4 Outputs from the preparation stage

- `00_critic_source_map.md` — useful sources, access status, strengths, and limitations.
- `00_critic_notebook.md` — a concise vocabulary and set of questions developed from the sources.
- `00_access_gaps.md` — unresolved access questions, normally requiring no human action.

The critic notebook is a lens, not a scoring sheet. It will explicitly reject simplistic claims such as “blue always means trust” or “red always converts.” Color and every other design choice will be interpreted in its particular composition, message, audience, and cultural setting.

## 3. Running the two audience tracks

The recommended default is two parallel but independent processes:

- **M track:** one men's winner and one men's loser.
- **W track:** one women's winner and one women's loser.

Each track gets its own critic and its own report. The critics begin from the same preparation notebook but do not begin by trying to make the audiences agree.

The primary agent acts as editor: it checks the data context, inspects the ads itself, challenges each report, and notes cross-audience relationships only where they feel earned.

If only one campaign contains a genuinely clear or useful next pair, the other track waits. Parallelism is an option, not a production target.

## 4. Selecting one pair

The numbers are used narrowly: to find a winner and loser whose difference is clear enough to deserve interpretation. The primary performance window is the ad's **first full, intentional week**. Leads and CPL in that week determine the label. An ad does not become a loser merely because it needs to be switched after a productive opening week.

Lifetime performance, later fatigue, reach, impressions, frequency, and engagement are supporting context only. Reach and frequency may help assess whether an ad received a fair opportunity or whether prior exposure is a plausible explanation; they do not replace leads as the outcome.

A candidate should have:

- enough spend or repeated delivery that the label is not absurd;
- a reasonably clear performance difference from its counterpart;
- no obvious data or intended-run problem that explains the result by itself;
- enough creative interest to make the comparison worthwhile.

The pair need not be the mathematical best and worst ads. A slightly less extreme pair may be more revealing if the ads share an idea, format, period, or other basis for comparison.

An ad with negligible delivery is “untested,” not a creative loser.

At the beginning of each round, the chosen pair and the minimal performance rationale will be shown. If the human objects to the selection, the pair changes before the criticism proceeds.

## 5. What each pair report will examine

Each report will be an illustrated comparative essay, not two independent scorecards.

### 5.1 The encounter

- What is apprehended in the first moment at phone size?
- Where does the eye go, and what becomes clear next?
- What feeling or proposition is available before the ad is read carefully?
- Does a stranger see an invitation, a martial-arts demonstration, a wellness poster, an obscure technique, or something else?

### 5.2 Image, body, and scene

- Framing, crop, scale, focal point, figure–ground separation, balance, and negative space.
- Whether faces, bodies, touch, movement, instruction, and spatial relationships are legible.
- Gesture and emotional temperature: calm, effort, threat, care, mastery, intimacy, play, stiffness, awkwardness.
- Setting and texture: real dojo, outdoors, illustration, staged photography, stock-like polish, documentary credibility.
- Whether the image makes participation imaginable for the intended audience.

### 5.3 Color

Color is an explicit part of every report:

- dominant palette, hue relationships, saturation, brightness, and temperature;
- contrast and its control of attention;
- separation of body, clothing, text, and background;
- harmony or productive tension between the colors and the verbal message;
- emotional and cultural associations in this particular ad;
- consistency or difference from the dojo's recognizable visual world;
- whether the palette feels alive, calming, clinical, muddy, decorative, aggressive, generic, or distinctive;
- accessibility and legibility, especially at mobile size.

Color will not be treated as an isolated conversion variable. The question is what the color is doing in the composition.

### 5.4 Typography and layout

- Hebrew typeface character, weight, size, spacing, line breaks, and right-to-left flow.
- Reading order and hierarchy among image, headline, supporting text, and CTA.
- Alignment, margins, safe zones, visual rhythm, density, and empty space.
- Contrast against the underlying image and the effect of overlays or boxes.
- Whether the design looks deliberate, templated, amateur, overworked, elegant, or anonymous.
- Whether the arrangement survives feed cropping and a quick mobile glance.

### 5.5 Headline and primary text

- What exact promise, tension, question, or identity is offered?
- Whether the language is specific, believable, fresh, generic, therapeutic, martial, or evasive.
- Rhythm and naturalness in Hebrew, including gendered address.
- Whether the primary text deepens the ad or starts a different conversation.
- Barrier removal, evidence, benefits, and CTA.
- What is said too early, too late, too vaguely, or too insistently.

### 5.6 The complete ad

- Do visual design, headline, and primary text make one coherent imaginative offer?
- What makes the winner alive or persuasive?
- What makes the loser inert, obscure, unbelievable, generic, or mismatched?
- Is the losing idea poor, or is it a potentially good idea damaged by execution?
- What is worth borrowing from each ad, including the loser?

### 5.7 Critical residue and doubt

Each pair report will end with:

- two to four things the comparison makes newly visible;
- tensions or uncertainties that should not be smoothed into rules;
- non-creative explanations such as delivery, timing, familiarity, or chance;
- questions for the human's response;
- a note about what kind of work a creative director would want to see next.

## 6. Discussion is part of the method

After the M and W reports are delivered, work stops.

The human's reaction is not treated as approval paperwork. It is part of the criticism. The discussion may reveal:

- a misreading of Hebrew tone;
- something false about the actual dojo experience;
- an aesthetic preference that should or should not govern advertising;
- important production history;
- a stronger alternative interpretation;
- a question that the next pair should investigate.

The reports will be revised if the discussion changes the reading.

Only when the human asks to continue will another pair be selected.

## 7. How later pairs enlarge the creative conversation

The next pair is chosen partly for performance clarity and mainly because it deserves to be put on the wall next. It should offer a fresh visual or emotional problem, disclose an unexpected strength, or make a failure interesting. It is not selected as an experiment designed to validate an earlier interpretation.

It might be selected because it:

- opens a different imaginative territory from the winners already discussed;
- is a strong result whose appeal is not immediately obvious;
- is a weak result that nevertheless contains a beautiful, credible, or usable idea;
- reveals a new relationship among bodies, setting, color, typography, and language;
- makes the intended audience imaginable in a way the current wall does not;
- provokes a question a creative director would want answered by making or finding more work.

Earlier readings may be remembered, but the new work will not be reduced to evidence for or against them. Each pair should first be encountered as a complete ad with its own mood, intelligence, and failure.

Two living notes will be maintained:

- `M_creative_readings.md`
- `W_creative_readings.md`

A small `cross_audience_notes.md` will record only comparisons worth remembering. It need not become a theory.

## 8. Report structure and filenames

The preparation stage and first round use:

```text
outputs/creative_critique/
  00_critic_source_map.md
  00_critic_notebook.md
  00_access_gaps.md
  00_metric_note_first_week.md
  01_proposed_first_pairs.md
  02_M_pair_report.md
  03_W_pair_report.md
```

The two reports will share a clear visual structure but retain different voices and conclusions. Images will appear at useful size, with mobile-scale versions where revealing. Video reports will include the opening sequence and representative frames.

There is no automatic final report. When the human feels enough pairs have been discussed, the existing pair reports can be shaped into creative direction. Until then, the reports remain a record of an evolving conversation.

## 9. Independent and outside criticism

For the first round, the preferred arrangement is:

- one independently prepared critic for M;
- one independently prepared critic for W;
- the primary agent as editor and cross-reader.

This uses parallel agents for separation of perspective, not speed. Their instructions and source notebook will be preserved.

If a report feels too obvious, culturally uncertain, or disputed, a third critic can receive the pair without performance information. A human expert packet can also be prepared, but the human will decide whom to contact and will send it.

AI critics will be described honestly as prepared independent readers, not credentialed human experts.

## 10. Tool and access judgment

The current tools can:

- read the repository's performance and copy data;
- inspect full-resolution images;
- extract and inspect video frames;
- search public web sources and the Meta Ad Library;
- prepare illustrated reports and expert packets;
- run independent agent readings.

No plugin is required to begin. Research-oriented plugins such as Consensus, SciSpace, or Sider Scholar may be useful if the source search turns into a substantial academic literature review, but they should be considered only after the source map identifies a real access problem. Canva or Figma would become relevant only if criticism turns into layout production.

## 11. Boundaries and approval

Approval authorizes:

- Stage 0 source mapping and critic preparation;
- an access-gap note that defaults to no human action;
- selection of the first M and W pairs for the human's confirmation;
- use of two independent critic agents after the pairs are confirmed;
- creation of new criticism artifacts without overwriting existing work.

Approval does not authorize changing campaigns, Grist data, existing decision logs, or contacting outside people.

The process will pause twice before any second round:

1. after Stage 0 and the proposed first-pair selections;
2. after the two first-pair reports and discussion.
