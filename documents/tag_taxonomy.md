# Tag Taxonomy – Ad Components

This document defines the allowed tag categories and their intended meaning. Tags are used for analysis, comparison, and hypothesis generation—not as guarantees of causality. It is legitimate to suggest new tags, but make sure this is done explicitly so that this file can be updated.

---

## General rules

* Tags should be **descriptive**, not aspirational.
* Tags describe what is *present*, not what was intended.

## Media tags

### Media style

Describes the visual character of the media.

Examples:

* candid
* instructional
* posed
* documentary
* dynamic
* quiet

### Media energy

Describes perceived movement and intensity.

Examples:

* low
* medium
* high

---

## Textual intent tags

### Hooks

What initially captures attention.

Examples:

* curiosity
* problem-naming
* identity
* reassurance
* novelty

### Promises

What benefit is implicitly or explicitly offered.

Examples:

* confidence
* physical competence
* calm
* strength
* learning

---

## Tone and structure

### Tone

How the text speaks.

Examples:

* direct
* reassuring
* reflective
* pragmatic

### Structure

How information is organized.

Examples:

* declarative
* question-led
* bullet-based
* narrative

---

## Language and audience

### Gendered grammar

Whether the language is explicitly gendered.

Values:

* masculine
* feminine
* neutral

### Target

Intended audience framing.

Examples:

* men
* women
* mixed

---

## Analytical notes

* Tags are hypotheses about why something works or does not work.
* A tag appearing in a successful ad does **not** imply causation.
* Strong tag-level conclusions require repetition across:

  * multiple ads
  * multiple weeks

When in doubt, leave a tag unassigned rather than guessing.
