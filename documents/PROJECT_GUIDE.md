# PROJECT GUIDE – Aikido Marketing

## 1. Project Scope

This project supports **both sponsored and organic content** for an Aikido dojo in Be’er Sheva.

The purpose of the project is to develop marketing content that creates **recognition, usefulness, and trust** over time, while also supporting effective lead generation when appropriate.

The project explicitly rejects hype, generic self‑improvement language, and martial‑arts bravado. Its goal is not persuasion by exaggeration, but invitation through clarity and credibility.

This document serves as the **governing reference** for all marketing‑related decisions in this project. When there is uncertainty or disagreement, this document takes precedence over ad‑hoc judgment.

**In scope**

* Organic posts that build presence, trust, and engagement
* Sponsored posts that invite trial participation via lead forms
* Clear differentiation between content types
* Consistent tone, structure, and decision rules

**Out of scope**

* Generic motivational content
* Martial‑arts marketing clichés
* Content optimized for virality at the expense of tone or credibility

If a post could plausibly belong to *any* dojo, it does not belong in this project.

---

## 2. Audience Framing

This project distinguishes audiences primarily by **content type** rather than by demographic micro‑segmentation.

### Why micro‑segmentation is avoided

Given the scale of the campaigns and the desired reach, micro‑segmentation provides little value and adds unnecessary complexity.

Sponsored campaigns are designed to:

* Reach audiences on the order of **10–30,000 people**
* Target broad age bands (e.g., women 25–35, men 30–40)
* Run as separate campaigns for men and women
* Operate on a budget of approximately **30 ILS per day per campaign**
* Reach a few thousand people per week, with each individual seeing the ad a small number of times

Under these conditions, further micro‑segmentation is a poor use of attention and effort.

Weekly sponsored-ad decisions use the men's and women's campaigns as separate two-slot systems. The process does not create separate rule sets inside each gender. It compares ads by observed performance, keeps short-term winners, replaces weak or non-delivering ads after enough current-run spend, reuses same-gender historical winners when they are strong enough, and creates complete new ads often enough to keep finding week-one winners.

For organic posts, the audience cannot be controlled in any case. The page has a few hundred followers, and organic reach depends on platform dynamics rather than targeting choices. As a result, micro‑segmentation has no meaningful logic for organic content.

### Sponsored content audience

* People with little or no prior knowledge of aikido
* Often encountering the dojo for the first time
* Primary question: *Is this for me?*

### Organic content audience

* Current students
* Friends and acquaintances of students
* Aikido‑adjacent or movement‑curious readers

**Secondary organic audience**

* Followers who arrived via sponsored posts and continue to see organic content

Organic content assumes some shared context. Sponsored content does not.

## 3. Marketing Guidelines

### 3.1 AIDA Framework

All content follows the **AIDA framework** (Attention / Interest / Desire / Action).

The key distinction between sponsored and organic content is the meaning of **Action**, and therefore how Desire is constructed.

* **Sponsored Action:** Fill out a lead form
* **Organic Action:** Engagement

For organic posts, AIDA should be applied **mentally rather than mechanically**, to avoid formulaic or ad‑like content.

**Organic engagement priorities (descending order):**

1. Commenting and sharing
2. Non‑thumbs‑up reactions
3. Likes

---

### 3.2 Sponsored Posts – Principles

**Primary goal:** Conversion

**Design priorities:**

* Clarity
* Speed
* Low friction

Sponsored posts are allowed—and expected—to be **formulaic**. Novelty is not a value in sponsored content and is not a goal.

In the weekly paid-ad process, a complete new ad means new media, a new headline, and new primary text designed together. The media concept comes first; headline and text are written to match it.

#### Sponsored structure

1. Visual (image or short video)

   * Clear focal point
   * Legible at small sizes
2. Headline

   * Identifies relevance
   * Makes *one* promise only
3. Subhead (optional)

   * Clarifies tone or removes anxiety
4. Body text

   * Short and direct
   * Often bullet‑based
   * Focused on benefits of aikido *as practiced here*
5. Call to action

   * Explicit and unambiguous
   * Low commitment (trial lesson, no obligation)

#### Sponsored tone

* Clear
* Reassuring
* Direct
* Purposeful

Avoid:

* Reflection or ambiguity
* Poetic or exploratory language
* Anything that slows decision‑making

If a sentence does not help someone decide whether to click, it does not belong.

---

### 3.3 Organic Posts – Principles

**Primary goal:** Engagement over time

Organic posts allow flexibility in structure and tone, but **must always include an explicit call for engagement**.

#### Organic structure

Organic posts typically operate in one (or more) of the following modes:

* **Teach** something concrete
* **Reframe** a familiar experience
* **Name** an experience the reader may recognize
* **Share** a personal experience or a dojo event that had meaning

Organic posts typically include:

1. Headline

   * Provocative question or claim
   * Justifies the post’s existence
2. Body

   * Directly addresses the headline
   * Delivers usable value:

     * Insight
     * Reframing
     * Practical advice
     * Experiential knowledge
3. Medium‑specific placement

   * Video: core idea in audio; caption reinforces or expands
   * Image: text in the body of the post
4. Call for engagement

   * Always present
   * Invites comments, sharing, or reactions
   * Framed as conversation, not promotion

#### Organic tone

* Observational and reflective
* Precise and grounded
* Comfortable with incompleteness
* Never a disguised ad
* **Blunt and confident about the real advantages of Aikido**

Organic posts are allowed to be blunt about *what Aikido offers*, but must not be instrumental about conversion. They may also simply be interesting, informative,  or useful. Engagement is the key.

---

### 3.4 Shared Tone and Language Constraints

Applies to all content unless explicitly overridden.

* Adult, calm, confident
* Understated rather than emphatic
* Short sentences preferred
* Emotional content usually implied, but may occasionally be declared

Avoid:

* Motivational clichés
* Moralizing language
* Martial bravado
* Over‑promising outcomes

---

### 3.5 Hebrew Writing Guidelines

* Prefer spoken Hebrew cadence
* Line breaks matter more than punctuation
* Short lines read as confident, not abrupt
* Avoid military, therapeutic, or self‑help jargon
* If something sounds "יפה מדי", simplify it

Sponsored copy should be read aloud once before approval.
Organic copy should be read once and then shortened.

---

### 3.6 Visual Principles

**Organic visuals**

* Can be quiet or rough
* Mood and authenticity over polish

**Sponsored visuals**

* Consistent style within a campaign
* One idea per image
* Immediate legibility

Experimentation belongs primarily in organic content.

## 4. Workflow & Execution

This section governs *how* content is produced and approved. Its purpose is to **reduce friction and second‑guessing**, not to slow down posting or introduce bureaucracy.

### 4.1 Content labeling

Every task should begin with an explicit label:

* `POST TYPE: ORGANIC`
* `POST TYPE: SPONSORED`

This label determines tone, structure, and success criteria.

---

### 4.2 Pre‑Publish Checklists

Both the assistant and the project owner should run a checklist before publishing. These checklists are related but not identical.

#### Assistant checklist – Sponsored post

Confirm that:

1. The target audience is clear and unfamiliar with aikido
2. There is exactly one core promise
3. The post is legible within seconds
4. The call to action is explicit and low‑friction
5. There is no reflective or exploratory language

#### Assistant checklist – Organic post

Confirm that:

1. There is a clear question or claim
2. The post delivers usable value
3. Advantages of Aikido are stated confidently but without selling
4. The assumed audience is aikido‑adjacent
5. There is an explicit invitation to engage

#### Owner checklist – Sponsored post

Confirm that:

1. The promise is accurate and defensible
2. The tone matches the dojo and the instructor
3. The visual and text feel consistent with the broader campaign
4. The CTA matches the actual onboarding experience

#### Owner checklist – Organic post

Confirm that:

1. The post sounds natural in your own voice
2. You would be comfortable standing behind it long‑term
3. It contributes to the public picture you want to build of the dojo
4. The engagement prompt feels genuine, not forced

---

### 4.3 Workflow Defaults

**Sponsored**

1. Identify the audience
2. Identify one promise
3. Build a clean AIDA chain
4. Cut anything extra

**Organic**

1. Identify a real observation
2. Frame it as a question or claim
3. Deliver value
4. Invite engagement explicitly

---

## 5. What This Project Is Not

* Not a generic self‑improvement brand
* Not a martial‑arts hype channel
* Not optimized for virality at the expense of tone or credibility
