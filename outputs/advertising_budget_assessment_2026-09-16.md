# Advertising budget and growth plan — 2026-09-16

## The short answer

We have two goals:

1. Grow by one or two students a month after replacing the students who cancel.
2. Build a more balanced dojo in which women make up at least 30% of active members, and ideally 40%.

The earlier plan put 70% of the advertising budget into the men's campaign because registrations from that campaign have cost less. It kept 30% for the women's campaign, but that would not do enough to support the second goal. At the results we have seen so far, only about 17% of registrations produced by that split would come through the women's campaign.

I therefore recommend starting with an **even split between the men's and women's campaigns**:

| Goal | Suggested monthly budget | Men's campaign | Women's campaign | Approximate daily budget |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Gain about 1 student per month | **₪3,300** | ₪1,650 | ₪1,650 | about ₪54/day for each campaign |
| Gain about 2 students per month | **₪4,850** | ₪2,425 | ₪2,425 | ₪80/day for each campaign |

For the ₪3,300 plan, each campaign receives ₪1,650 per month. That is **₪54.25 per day per campaign** over an average calendar month. A simple whole-number setting of ₪54/day each will average about ₪3,285 per month in total; ₪55/day each equals ₪3,300 in a 30-day month.

Start with the ₪3,300 plan for two full months. Then review how many men and women reached a trial, joined, and remained active. Move toward the larger budget only if the extra inquiries are turning into suitable long-term students.

## Why this is more than a marketing target

Reaching at least 30% female membership—and ideally 40%—is a goal about what kind of dojo we want to be, not just which ads are cheapest. It matters even if recruiting women currently costs more.

Gender is recorded in the Students table. Among the 21 active, non-Guest students, 5 are women and 16 are men. Women therefore make up **23.8% of active membership**. There is also one paused student, who is a woman; if paused membership is included, women are 6 of 22 students, or **27.3%**.

Because the goal is stated in terms of active membership, this report uses 23.8% as the starting point. If nobody else joined or left, 2 additional active women would bring the dojo to 7 of 23, or 30.4%. Six additional active women would bring it to 11 of 27, or 40.7%. In practice, men will also join and students will cancel, so registrations, returns, and cancellations should continue to be counted separately by gender each month.

## How the gender goal changes the advertising plan

Recent results suggest that a new student from the women's campaign has cost more than a new student from the men's campaign. Based on those results, the likely mix of registrations produced by ads looks like this:

| Advertising approach | Men / women budget split | Estimated registrations coming through the women's campaign | Working budget for +1 student/month | Working budget for +2 students/month |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Earlier cost-led mix | 70% / 30% | about 17% | ₪2,900 | ₪4,250 |
| **Recommended balanced start** | **50% / 50%** | **about 32%** | **₪3,300** | **₪4,850** |
| Stronger push toward balance | 40% / 60% | about 41% | ₪3,600 | ₪5,200 |

These percentages are guides, not promises. They are based on only six recent registrations that could be clearly tied to a campaign: four from the men's campaign and two from the women's campaign. The campaign used is not proof of a student's gender. The women's results could also improve substantially if more women are contacted promptly and helped to attend a trial.

An even split is therefore a sensible starting test. At the current results, it would make about 32% of registrations come through the women's campaign without immediately moving to the most expensive option. We must still verify how many of those students are women. Because active female membership currently stands at 23.8%, a 50/50 advertising split may move the overall percentage slowly. If two months of results are not clearly closing the gap, move closer to a 40% men / 60% women split.

## Why the current budget is not producing steady growth

From March through August 2026:

- 9 students registered.
- 7 students cancelled.
- The recorded gain was 2 students over six months, or about one-third of a student per month.
- Cancellations averaged about 1.2 students per month.

Current advertising spend is about **₪1,800–₪1,900 per month**. At the recent results, that is enough for roughly 1.2 registrations from ads per month. In other words, the current advertising is mostly replacing cancellations rather than producing clear growth.

The working budgets do not assume that the students who previously joined without being linked to an ad will continue to arrive at the same rate. That makes them more cautious than the bare-minimum estimate, but they do not include an extra safety margin beyond the stated growth goal. The main uncertainties are:

- some students join without being linked to a particular ad;
- attendance records and membership records do not always move together;
- advertising results vary from month to month.

This is why the recommended amount is higher than the bare-minimum estimate. It is still only a planning estimate. If advertising becomes more expensive or the gap between attendance and membership records continues, more money may be needed.

## Is the main problem cancellation?

Cancellation is part of the problem, but it does not appear to be the largest problem.

The earlier analysis treated students who stopped attending for a month as if they had permanently cancelled. That was incorrect: some paused and later returned. The corrected records show 7 actual cancellations from March through August, not 12.

Using the previous month's attendance count gives a rough cancellation percentage of about 6.5%. This is only an estimate because attendance and paid membership are not the same thing. For planning the budget, the more useful number is the actual average of **about 1.2 recorded cancellations per month**.

## Where prospective students are being lost

The larger weakness is what happens between an inquiry and an attended trial, especially in the women's campaign.

| Step | Men's campaign | Women's campaign | Combined |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Inquiries recorded | 133 | 125 | 258 |
| Had a phone or appointment contact | 33 | 14 | 47 |
| Attended a trial | 15 | 6 | 21 |
| Registered | 9 | 3 | 12 |
| Percentage of inquiries that registered | 6.8% | 2.4% | 4.7% |

Only 21 of 258 inquiries have a recorded trial, while 12 registrations are recorded. These are separate summary counts, so they do not prove that 12 of those exact 21 trial students registered. They do show that too few inquiries are reaching a recorded trial, or that trials are not always being recorded.

The women's campaign needs particular attention. It generated almost as many inquiries as the men's campaign, but far fewer appointment contacts, trials, and registrations. Spending more on the women's campaign without improving these steps would be expensive and would not necessarily create a more balanced membership.

## What to improve first

1. **Reply quickly.** Set a clear target for how soon a new inquiry receives a personal response.
2. **Use a consistent follow-up routine.** Decide when to call or message again if there is no answer, rather than handling each inquiry differently.
3. **Make trials easier to attend.** Send an immediate confirmation, a reminder the day before, and a short same-day message.
4. **Make the women's ads and forms clearer.** State who the class is for, the Be'er Sheva location, the schedule, and what a first class feels like. This may reduce unsuitable inquiries and make the right people more comfortable attending.
5. **Ask women who did not attend why.** A small number of short conversations may reveal practical or cultural barriers that the current records cannot show.
6. **Track whether people stay.** Check whether new students are still active after 30, 60, and 90 days, separately for men and women.

## A practical two-month plan

For the next two complete months:

- Spend about **₪3,300 per month**, split evenly between the men's and women's campaigns.
- Keep the weekly ad decisions based on the actual cost of getting an inquiry, but do not let short-term cost alone override the female-membership goal.
- Use the current active baseline of 5 women and 16 men, and keep the `Sex` field complete as students join, return, pause, or cancel.
- Each month, count inquiries, trials, registrations, cancellations, and active members separately for men and women.
- After two months, keep the 50/50 split if women's share of active membership is moving toward 30%. Move toward 40% men / 60% women if it is not.
- Consider the ₪4,850 plan only after the dojo can handle the extra inquiries and the follow-up process is working consistently.

## Important limits of this estimate

- Some registrations cannot be linked to a particular advertisement.
- Attendance counts and actual membership status are different; the report does not treat a missed month as an automatic cancellation.
- The current gender breakdown is known, but how long men and women remain active still needs to be tracked consistently.
- Student fees, average length of membership, and other costs are not available. This report estimates the advertising needed to gain students; it does not show whether that spending is profitable.
